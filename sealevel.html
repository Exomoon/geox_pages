PalaeoCoast — Experimental Geospatial Reconstruction

How did European coastlines transform as the last ice age retreated? PalaeoCoast is an experimental geospatial research project exploring how prehistoric landscapes can be reconstructed by coupling bathymetry with relative sea-level curves, topological ocean connectivity, and geomorphological processes. Powered by the computational engine SeaLevelRize.

Understanding Dynamic Land Motion

Simply flooding today's bathymetry ignores millennia of tectonic and isostatic adjustment. Real landscapes evolve in 3 dimensions.

↑

Vertical Uplift (Isostasy & Tectonics)

When bedrock rebounded post-glacially, ancient land was lower than today. The model inverts this cumulative displacement to restore authentic historical terrain heights.

↓

Subsidence & Basin Sinking

In zones with sediment compaction or tectonic stretching, land surfaces have subsided. Ancient coastal plains were higher than present bathymetric soundings.

⨀

Enclosed Depressions vs True Ocean

Low-lying ground below sea level stays dry land if shielded by a physical sill. The engine computes 4-way topological ocean connectivity, preventing premature inland flooding.

Ancient Elevation = Modern Elevation − U (land motion) + E (erosion) − D (deposition)
Active Development

Numerical GIA Engine for Fennoscandia & Northern Europe

The current viewer illustrates the computational principle using synthetic test gradients across the Aegean. For Northern Europe and Doggerland, linear approximations are insufficient — geophysical Glacial Isostatic Adjustment (GIA) is required to simulate mantle viscosity, postglacial crustal rebound, and peripheral forebulge collapse. This module is actively under development, harmonizing empirical ice margin data (DATED-1/BRITICE) with geodetic rebound models (NKG2016LU).

Historical Epochs & Model Limitations

The engine models geometric elevation scenarios for primary horizons. It serves as a methodological testbed and scenario platform — not a finalized regional geodynamic ground truth.

22 000 BP · Glacial Maximum

Exposed Continental Shelf

Global oceans dropped ~120–125m below modern datum. The Cyclades merged into a contiguous landmass and Doggerland formed an extensive plain connecting Britain to mainland Europe.
11 700 BP · Younger Dryas / Holocene

Rapid Marine Transgression

Abrupt deglaciation drove surging sea levels. Marine sills breached, fragmenting land bridges into the archipelago of islands and channels we observe today.
0 BP · 1950 Reference Baseline

Modern Calibration Benchmark

Modern GEBCO and EMODnet baseline where cumulative process displacement equals zero. Used as the modern geodetic control surface.
